Category: Non-Google
Status: Released (v1.0b)

Sources:

- [Release record and credit](https://schema.org/docs/releases.html#v1.0b)

## 2014-02-05 — Offers broadened for libraries

Category: Non-Google
Status: Released (v1.0f)
Summary: **Dan Scott and Schema Bib Extend** broaden offer descriptions to cover nonprofit use cases such as library lending.

This changes the scope of the model, rather than merely fixing spelling: an item can be offered without being sold.

Sources:

- [Release record and credits](https://schema.org/docs/releases.html#v1.0f)

## 2014-08-18 — Periodicals and bibliographic relationships

Category: Non-Google
Status: Released (v1.9)
Summary: **Richard Wallis (OCLC), Dan Scott (Laurentian) and Bib Extend** contribute periodicals, issues, volumes and work/example relationships.

The release credits Wallis as chair and Scott for implementation. The September 2 guest post explains the released work. Persistent acknowledgements also identify inspiration from the Bibliographic Ontology (BIBO).

Sources:

- [Guest post](https://blog.schema.org/2014/09/02/schema-org-support-for-bibliographic-relationships-and-periodicals/)
- [Bibliographic acknowledgement](https://github.com/schemaorg/schemaorg/blob/main/data/collab/bibex.md)
- [Release record](https://schema.org/docs/releases.html#v1.9)

## 2015-08-06 — Bibliographic hosted extension

Category: Non-Google
Status: Hosted extension preview (v2.1)
Summary: **The bibliographic community** gains `bib.schema.org` for more specialized vocabulary within the hosted-extension mechanism.

The extension was offered for final review in v2.1 and finalized in v3.0 on May 4, 2016. Hosted extensions remained part of Schema.org's release process.

Sources:

- [v2.1 release](https://schema.org/docs/releases.html#v2.1)
- [v3.0 finalization](https://schema.org/docs/releases.html#v3.0)

## 2019-04-01 — Archives and further creative-work types

Category: Non-Google
Status: Released (v3.5; new terms)
Summary: **Richard Wallis and bibliographic contributors** extend coverage of archives, manuscripts, sheet music, plays and other creative works.

Git directly credits Wallis for the creative-work type additions. Archive-related proposals are separately tracked as #1758 and #1759; this should not imply he originated all archive work.
